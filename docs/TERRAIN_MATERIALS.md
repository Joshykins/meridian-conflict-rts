# Terrain materials and forest fire presentation

Terrain uses a mipmapped RGBA8 array at scene binding 18: ten CC0 ground scans (cliff rock, lush grass, aerial meadow, mossy litter, needle forest floor, scree, dry dirt, aerial rocky highland, beach gravel, mud), two layers each (albedo/roughness, then normal XY/height/occlusion), followed by the foliage and bark layers at `FOLIAGE_BASE`. Asset credits, channel layouts, checksums and the reproducible import command are in `data/textures/terrain/README.md`.

Three randomly rotated and offset patches are blended on a triangular lattice within each projection. Shared lattice corners preserve transforms across boundaries. Explicit UV gradients prevent random offsets from corrupting mip selection. A bounded five-step parallax trace adds relief up close to the dominant material.

Every ground pixel weighs all nine non-cliff materials from world-space fields: moisture (740 m), habitats (310 m), soil patches (46 m), a 27/61 m patchwork of grass types, altitude, slope, curvature (hollows are wet, ridges dry), shore distance and forest cover. The three strongest are sampled from above, the fourth's weight is subtracted so a material fades before it is dropped, and the survivors are height-blended so stones and tufts of one poke through another. Each scan is tinted to one palette (`ground_tint`). Cliffs keep the triplanar rock face. A 42 m luminance layer from the aerial meadow scan keeps ground from flattening into one colour at battle-camera distances.

Forest cover (binding 20, `ground_cover.rs`) is an RG8 map built at load from the map's trees: canopy closure and conifer share, blurred ~10 m past the trunks, 8 m texels on small maps and up to 4096 texels on an edge. Needle floor lies under conifers, mossy litter under broadleaf canopy and along edges, and closed canopy darkens the floor even where the shadow map has faded out.

Relief below the 8 m heightfield comes from three analytic-derivative noise octaves (34, 9.5, 2.8 m), rougher on stony ground and smoother on sand and mud, with fine octaves fading as pixels grow. Boulders (7 m jittered grid, rock scan greyed like the cliffs, domed normals and contact shadow) appear only on gentle stony ground. Material cavity occlusion, terrain concavity and four horizon-shadow samples add depth. Heightfield geometry, collision and navigation are unchanged.

The scene uses reduced sunlight, sky fill and exposure. Rock props share terrain materials. Trees use six layers from `FOLIAGE_BASE` (`foliage.rs`, credits and layout in `data/textures/foliage/README.md`): a broadleaf and a conifer leaf atlas composed offline from CC0 leaf, fir-twig and pine-needle scans, and two scanned barks (albedo/roughness plus normal/occlusion). Broadleaf crowns are irregular domes of leafy lobes on a flared, forking trunk; firs are drooping whorls of flat fronds around a dark core to a narrow spire; pines hold an umbrella of needle pads over a tall bare trunk; dead trees are broken snags. Every leaf card (`MeshBuilder::leaf_card`) picks an atlas region by UV and carries its crown's outward normal and depth, so crowns light as lumps of foliage, darken toward the heart and underside, glow when backlit, and vary in hue per tree and per card. Visible and shadow passes use the same alpha test; leaves sway in wind and burn away in small groups, exposing the branches. Levels of detail run about 330–580, 110–160 and 20–28 triangles. The fire shader uses advected noise for irregular flames instead of repeated candle shapes.

A newly destroyed tree beside a ground splash impact becomes a presentation emitter. The static tree is culled by its existing dead bit, while a temporary dynamic copy chars over nine seconds. Flames subside over thirty seconds, smoke continues, and the crown collapses between thirty-four and forty-two seconds. At most 256 trees are retained, with reserved dynamic-buffer capacity. Reclaim, construction clearing, shield interception and destruction already present on the first render frame do not ignite trees. Fire does not spread or add gameplay damage, and does not change simulation or replay state.

Checks:

- `cargo test -p mc-render textures::tests` validates material sizes, normal vectors, roughness/occlusion channels, noise seams and mipmaps.
- `cargo test --release -p mc-render forest_fire_lifecycle_and_render -- --ignored --nocapture` uses a real Vulkan device and `maps/dev16.mcmap`, verifies reclaim/shield exclusions, ignition, non-retriggering, dynamic tree retention and expiry, and captures live foliage, fire, cliffs, close rock relief and grass in `artifacts/terrain-v2/`.
- `cargo test -p mc-render tree_canopies` verifies cutout geometry, atlas regions, crown shading data, triangle winding and LOD budgets; `cargo test -p mc-render foliage::` checks the leaf atlases (air gaps, bled colour, coverage-preserving mips) and bark normals.
- `cargo check -p mc-game` compiles and validates WGSL through naga as part of the renderer build.

The full renderer suite during this change passed 63 tests and reported three model failures outside this work: shield tech2 height, gunship mesh IDs, and factory_land LOD reduction. The focused material tests and the Windows GPU fire test passed.
