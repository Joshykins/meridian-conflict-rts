This audit backs the rules in `CLAUDE.md`. It covers about 222k lines of Rust in 12
crates and 17k lines of WGSL. Method:
- clippy across the workspace (except mc-studio, see T1)
- rustfmt
- four read-only sweeps: determinism, dead code, CPU-GPU contracts, and structure/safety
- a naga layout check of every shader

File:line references are as of this date.

## Overall

The engine's design is sound, and a few things are better than usual:
- No layer depends upward: mc-sim never sees the renderer or the network.
- Determinism has real tests: identical hashes at 0/1/3/8 workers, and snapshot then same future.
- Network decoding length-checks every read.
- There are no TODOs and no commented-out code.
- The Rust and WGSL struct sizes all agree today.

The weakness is that almost nothing is enforced. Nothing checks the rules in
ARCHITECTURE.md; people keep them. There is no pinned toolchain, no lint
config, no CI, no format config and no crate-level `deny`/`forbid`. Contracts
between Rust and WGSL agree by convention only. As the tree grows and more
sessions work in it at once, convention is what breaks first.

## Findings

### Process (highest leverage)

- **P1. Almost nothing is committed.**
  - There are 5 commits in total.
  - The working tree holds about 18k changed lines in 180 files, plus 107 untracked paths, including whole crates (`mc-music`, `mc-listen`).
  - Parallel sessions share one tree with no branch isolation, so one bad edit or `git checkout` can lose days of work.
  - Nothing can be bisected.
- **P2. No gates.** Nothing stops a commit that doesn't build, has warnings or breaks a test.

### Tooling

- **T1. Toolchain not pinned.**
  - WSL has rustc 1.90.
  - mc-studio's egui 0.36 needs 1.95, so `cargo clippy --workspace` fails on Linux.
  - Windows and WSL may be on different compilers.
- **T2. rustfmt drift.** 255 of 398 `.rs` files differ from `cargo fmt` defaults. There is no `rustfmt.toml`, and 3,834 lines are over 100 columns, so the house style is wider than the default.
- **T3. Clippy.** About 280 warnings outside `unreachable_pub`: 31 too-many-arguments, 22 dead-code, and the rest mechanical. Zero is reachable in a day, and then it can be denied. `unreachable_pub` adds 1,162, which is a measure of how much is `pub` for no reason.
- **T4. Manifests.**
  - mc-game, mc-studio and mc-listen use `path = "../..."` dependencies.
  - `serde_json` and `midir` are declared per crate.
  - mc-music, mc-studio and mc-listen are missing from the ARCHITECTURE crate table.
  - The edition is 2021.

### Determinism

- **S1. Two float computations feed sim state.**
  - `combat.rs:1745-1746`: the rocket-rack flight tick count goes through `to_f32()`, `ceil`, `as i32`.
  - `titan.rs:117-122,431`: the sabot wreck heading comes from the f32 `tumble()`.
  - Both are probably stable across platforms, but they break rule 1 and nothing catches them.
- **S2. Tie-dependent sort.** In `economy.rs:331`, `sort_unstable_by_key(target)` followed by `dedup_by_key` keeps an arbitrary job among ties.
- **S3. Silent caps (rule 5).**
  - `orders.rs:723` (`.take(MAX_PATROL_POINTS)`)
  - `orders.rs:782`
  - `standing.rs:167,184`
  - `titan.rs:433` (a wreck skipped when the table is full)
  - `world.rs:278`
  - `debug.rs:189`
  - `air_support.rs:28`
  - `placement.rs:99` (`.ok()?`)
- **S4. Coverage gaps.**
  - The thread-count tests run a flat map with a land roster only.
  - Navy, air, nukes, the titan and survival have no cross-thread check.
  - There is no pinned golden hash, so a Windows-vs-Linux divergence would pass unseen.
- **S5. Presentation floats in mc-sim.** About 340 float lines sit in mc-sim, 180 of them in `mirror.rs`, the rest scattered: `print_heads`, `transport`, `survival`, `nukes`, `reclaim`, `repair`, `mines`, and `combat.rs:88-110,1960-1993`. They are harmless today, but they make a mechanical ban impossible until they are gathered into one presentation module.

### CPU-GPU contracts

- **C1. Offsets are unchecked.**
  - build.rs `check_strides` checks 12 WGSL sizes against hand-typed numbers.
  - Rust `size_of` asserts cover 15 structs.
  - Nothing compares field offsets.
  - Nothing checks `Globals` (8,608 bytes, the most-edited struct) or about 10 other structs.
- **C2. Bindings are unchecked.** The descriptor bindings in pipelines.rs:389-431 are not compared with the shaders' `@binding`s, and binding 15 lives outside bindings.wgsl.
- **C3. Bare literals in shaders.**
  - `ModelInfo.icon` flags: 11 bits packed in renderer.rs:1163-1182, several of them from mesh-name compares, and read as bare hex 29 times in entity.wgsl.
  - Part ids are written as literals (`in.part == 16u`, ...).
  - Material ids live in 3 places, and `material_of` switches on literal `case 0u..26u`.
  - The rig bits are overloaded: `HOUSE_HIGH` sits inside `UPGRADE_AT_MASK`, limb 4 means two things, and the WGSL side re-encodes the `WORK_*` bits.
- **C4. About 60 constants are hand-copied on both sides.** These include all 37 puff kinds (as f32 on the Rust side, across 7 files), `UNIT_PAUSED` (read via a field named `_pad3a`) and the Globals array lengths. One comment already disagrees with the code: nukes are described as "three vec4 each" but use 4.
- **C5. WGSL structs defined twice and already diverging.** `Shield` is in both entity.wgsl and shields.wgsl, where the same slot is `_pad0` in one and `prev_radius` in the other. `ShieldHit` and `Shockwave` are also defined twice.
- **C6. Enum order used as ids.**
  - `IconKind` has implicit discriminants and is matched in icons.wgsl `case 0u..28u`, so inserting a variant shifts every icon.
  - `PropKind::ALL` is a hand-kept list, so a variant left out of it is silently dropped.
  - `pass_kind` is written as literals in Rust.
- **C7. Behaviour keyed on names.**
  - mc-sim branches on the render mesh `"assault_air"` (combat.rs, 7 places) and on `"reclaim_drone"`.
  - `air_support.rs:126` spawns `"aster_t1_interceptor"`, which will break for Naga.
  - "Is this a Shatter gun" is decided by the sound name, in both mirror.rs and renderer.rs.
  - Six separate `match mesh` tables in models/mod.rs must all be edited for every new spaceship.

### Structure and safety

- **A1. God objects.**
  - `Renderer` has 193 fields.
  - The biggest functions are `prepare` (1,220 lines), `render` (1,108), `effects_of_inner` (901), `mirror::write_render_frame` (985) and `combat::step_weapon` (986).
  - `Game` has 70 fields and a 547-line `frame`.
  - `Hud` has 47 fields.
  - `World` is spread across 39 files as `impl World` blocks, so modules act as namespaces rather than boundaries.
- **A2. Nothing is encapsulated.**
  - 18 of `World`'s 20 fields are `pub`, including `state`, `nav` and `fog`.
  - mc-sim has 631 `pub` fields.
  - Any crate can mutate lockstep state. Today they only read it, but nothing enforces that.
- **A3. Unsound API.** `mc-game/src/loading.rs:24` has `unsafe impl<T> Send for Unsend<T>` with a safe `new`, so any `Rc` can cross threads. `mc-jobs ForState::new` has an unstated precondition.
- **A4. unsafe blocks are undocumented.** mc-render has 175 unsafe blocks and about 0 `// SAFETY:` comments. No crate forbids unsafe.
- **A5. GPU resources are not owned.**
  - `Buffer` and `Image` have no `Drop`.
  - `Renderer::drop` lists about 40 buffers by hand, so a new field leaks silently.
  - Each resource gets its own `vkAllocateMemory`.
- **A6. Unbounded decode of external bytes.**
  - Match options (setup.rs:1004, survival.rs:145,150) and snapshots (world.rs:990) use unlimited `bincode::deserialize`.
  - A restored `State` is checked for only two lengths.
  - There is no fuzz target for `Message`/`Command` decoding.
- **A7. Errors.**
  - mc-game uses `Result<_, String>` in 28 places.
  - There is no panic hook or crash log for a Windows GUI build.
  - `perf_budgets.rs` panics in plain debug `cargo test` unless an env var is set.

## Roadmap

Each phase is a separate commit series. The order matters: phase 0 makes
everything after it safe to do.

### Phase 0: baseline (an hour; must be done while no other session is editing)
1. Commit the current tree as it is. It is the most valuable thing on this list.
2. Fix README (D1).
3. Delete or ignore `zz_wharf_probe` (D5).
4. Untrack the `.pyc` file, ignore `__pycache__/`, and move the stray PNG (D7).

### Phase 1: gates (half a day)
1. Add `rust-toolchain.toml` pinned to a version that builds everything, which means at least 1.95 (T1).
2. Add `rustfmt.toml` matching the house width, then one format-only commit (T2).
3. Add `[workspace.lints]` with every crate opting in via `[lints] workspace = true`:
   - rust: `unsafe_op_in_unsafe_fn = "deny"`, `unreachable_pub = "warn"`, `unused_qualifications = "warn"`
   - clippy: `undocumented_unsafe_blocks = "warn"`, `allow_attributes_without_reason = "warn"`, `too_many_arguments` allowed at the workspace level (31 today; fix them over time)
4. Add `#![forbid(unsafe_code)]` to mc-core, mc-data, mc-map, mc-path, mc-sim and mc-net.
5. Add `scripts/check.sh`: `cargo fmt --check`, `cargo clippy --workspace --all-targets -- -D warnings`, `cargo test --workspace --release` (with the perf-budget env var). Install it as a git `pre-commit` hook (fmt + clippy) and a `pre-push` hook (tests).
6. Clear the ~280 clippy warnings and the D3 dead code, then switch `-D warnings` on.

### Phase 2: determinism (a day)
1. Fix S1 and S2.
2. Turn the S3 caps into errors.
3. Gather the presentation floats (S5) into `mc_sim::view`/`mirror`.
4. Add `crates/mc-sim/clippy.toml` and `crates/mc-path/clippy.toml` with `disallowed-types` (HashMap, HashSet, Instant, SystemTime) and `disallowed-methods` (`Instant::now`, `thread::current`, `available_parallelism`), plus `#![deny(clippy::float_arithmetic)]`, with one `allow` on the presentation module.
5. Add a source-scan test (`mc-sim/tests/rules.rs`) that greps sim and path sources for `f32|f64|to_f32|HashMap|Instant|thread::current|sort_unstable` outside an allowlist. It is toolchain-independent and catches `to_f32`, which clippy misses.
6. Build a determinism matrix: {flat land, a baked naval map, survival, nukes/titan} × {0, 1, 3, 8 workers} + snapshot/restore, with a pinned golden hash per scenario, run on Windows and WSL (S4).

### Phase 3: contracts (two days)
1. Move GPU ids, bits and lengths into `mc-render/src/gpu_consts.rs`, and have build.rs generate `generated.wgsl` from it into the shader prelude. Replace every bare literal and hand copy (C3, C4). Puff kinds become `u32` in Rust.
2. Add a layout test: naga layouter vs `size_of` + `offset_of!` for every uploaded struct pair, which replaces the hand-typed `check_strides` numbers (C1). A throwaway layout tool from this audit is a starting point.
3. Add a binding test: turn the pipeline layout table into a `const` array and compare it with every `@group/@binding` in naga's module (C2).
4. Move `Shield`, `ShieldHit` and `Shockwave` into the prelude once (C5).
5. Give `IconKind` explicit discriminants, test that the icons.wgsl case count equals the variant count, and derive `PropKind::ALL` or check it exhaustively (C6).

### Phase 4: safety (a day)
1. Make `Unsend::new` an `unsafe fn` (or restrict it to `Renderer`), and make `ForState::new` an `unsafe fn` (A3).
2. Add bounded `bincode::options().with_limit()` for options and snapshots, `State::validate()` after restore, and a `cargo fuzz` target for `Message`/`Command` decoding (A6).
3. Add a panic hook that writes a crash log next to the settings file (A7).
4. Add `// SAFETY:` comments across mc-render (A4). Once done, set `undocumented_unsafe_blocks` to deny.
5. Give buffers and images an owner, or a deletion queue, so `Renderer::drop` stops being a list (A5).

### Phase 5: structure (ongoing, done as you touch things)
1. Break `Renderer` into owned sub-structs: effects, upload, targets, record.
   - Split `prepare` and `render` per pass.
   - Split `step_weapon` into acquire/aim/fire/volley.
   - Split `orders.rs` into dispatch/air/build/factory.
   - Split `game.rs` into input/camera/sounds/commands.
   - Move `hud/mod.rs`'s mine overlay out.
2. Encapsulate `World`: make `state`, `nav` and `fog` `pub(crate)` with read accessors, so lockstep state changes only through `Command` (A2).
3. Replace the name checks with blueprint fields, and fold the `match mesh` tables into one traits record per model (C7).
4. Remove the parked Roost pieces and empty the four logistics icon arms (D4). Remove the bitmap font and `font8x8` (D2).
5. Add `docs/SWITCHES.md` listing every env var and flag, and a test that every `env::var("MERIDIAN_...")` in the source is listed there (D6).
6. Put all dependencies under `[workspace.dependencies]` (T4). Plan the move to edition 2024.
