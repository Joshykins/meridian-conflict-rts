# Meridian Conflict: Lore One-Pager

This is the **truth layer**. None of it is ever stated on screen. Players see only the surface: clues they piece together themselves. Names marked *(placeholder)* are still open; lines marked *(proposed)* are not yet agreed.

## The premise

Humanity got to the stars on its own. No one handed it anything. Its own crude, costly FTL drive made the first jump, the **Crossing**, in year **0 AC** (After Crossing) *(proposed anchor)*. Survey ships found **Asteria**, a perfect world. Earth and Asteria unified as **the Asterian Reach**, with **Asterian Reach Command (ARC)** as its military. Around 150 AC the Reach settled **Meridia**, a frontier ore world. Somewhere along the way, ARC's tech divisions found something that nobody could explain. It may have been a smaller installation on Asteria rather than Meridia itself *(user's maybe)*. They classified it and started digging. **Humanity knows nothing about what it has found,** and the public doesn't even know that: the tech divisions are secretive, and until the Arrival no one outside them has any reason to believe aliens exist.

**The secret:** the **Precursors** *(placeholder)* built worlds. Asteria is one of their gardens. Meridia is their **Foundry**: a world-sized machine with a planet's crust grown over it. **The Meridian** is the seam, pole to pole, where its shell breaks the surface. The **Progenitor** (in code, the replication engine) is the Foundry's working machinery, and its **Shapers** are the lesser machines it raises. It is still carrying out its last orders, and nobody is left to stop it.

**Human fabrication is stolen, late and desperate.** Printing, reclaim and the Commander don't exist when the war starts. ARC's tech divisions reverse-engineer them from the ruins in the war years, because conventional industry can't replace losses fast enough. They half-understand what they copy.

**The Regency knows more.** Long ago the Regency's people were rival houses at war. One house found a Precursor machine *(ship or star core: open)*, took it apart, and reverse-engineered enough from it to win the war and bind the other houses into one empire. They did this thousands of years before humans tried the same thing. Their technology is their own, built from what they learned: star cores (gravity-pinched fusion), plasma weapons, nanites and shields. They don't run on Precursor machines. The tech itself is fixed in STYLE.md, "The Naga suite" (`naga` stays the code label). A house's rank still rests on the relics it holds and understands.

**Why they are a regency** *(proposed)*. In all those thousands of years, the gap has never closed. Their star cores are crude copies of originals they can describe but not rebuild. The relics the houses hold are still better than anything the Regency makes, and Precursor works (garden worlds, ruins that mend themselves) still keep running with no one tending them. A people who built everything they have from fragments, and know it, don't call the makers their equals. So the founding house didn't crown itself. It took the honest title: regent, ruling the estate the true **Sovereigns** (the Regency's name for the Precursors) left behind, until they return. The Regent is the head of state. Houses compete to be Regent, and doubters say the Sovereigns are gone for good. It's reverence built on evidence, not a faith: they are not zealots.

**Like the Covenant, they know more and get it wrong** *(proposed)*. The whole state rests on one reading: the Sovereigns *left*, the worlds are an estate *held* for them, and one day they will *return*. It's a reasonable reading, and it is partly wrong. What's really true is open; it pays off when the Foundry wakes. Never let the Foundry answer humans as the rightful heirs: that premise was rejected.

**Why the war starts** *(proposed)*: the best human colony worlds, the ones where Earth wheat took the first season, are Sovereign gardens. Humans settled them because they were strangely easy to live on, and never asked why. To the Regency, humanity has spread through the Sovereigns' estate: living on it, strip-mining its works to copy them in a hurry. Reverse-engineering isn't the offence, since the Regency did it too. The offence is where and how: on the estate itself, destroying originals that can't be replaced.

Then, at the end of Era II, the digging on Meridia wakes something that signals. For the first time in the Regency's history, a Sovereign work *does* something, and human diggers are standing on it. To the Regency it may be the return they have waited for, and the estate is being looted on the day. In 175 AC its envoys come to Meridia and demand that humans leave, which is lawful by the Regency's founding law. The rebels, who have been looting the ruins, fire on them. To the Regency all humans are one people, so ARC takes the blame.

The Regency doesn't set out to kill a species. But humanity's best worlds are all on the estate: for the Regency, eviction is reclaiming what it holds in trust; for humans, it's losing nearly everything. Neither side can give way, and the Regent's standing at home means no Regent can back down. It becomes total war without hatred: humans are in the way of a duty older than they are.

**Glassing** isn't the reason for the war. The beams are excavation tools: glassing burns off what humans built and lays bare the Sovereigns' work beneath. It targets Sovereign worlds that won't yield, and anywhere humans hold what they built from stolen Precursor works. Worlds that aren't Sovereign-built, such as Earth and Hale, are left alone unless they hold that tech; Hale is besieged for ARC's fabrication yards *(proposed)*.

## Worlds

**Earth** (old, political) · **Asteria** (garden capital, the last stand) · **Hale** (ice mining; Serac maps) · **an ocean colony** (naval; Twin Shoals; the first world glassed) · **a desert moon** (the rebels' heartland) · **Meridia** (the Foundry; Meridian Basin, The Threshold).

## The campaign timeline

The campaign opens with humans fighting humans on a colony world, using conventional forces: no printing, no Commander. The Regency arrives, and from then on it's setback after setback: billions dead, worlds falling. The player's tech ladder follows the story. Printing and the Commander arrive in the war, as the tech divisions' desperate prototypes.

**Tone:** Halo's Fall of Reach. Humanity is gritty and self-made, and everything Precursor it ends up holding, it stole and half-understands. ARC is the superpower in Era II, so the player feels strong. Then the Regency hits far harder, fast. From the Arrival on, the war is desperate: objectives are hold, evacuate, delay, lives saved. Hope comes from **small wins**: one ship, one city, one more month. Each one is earned and each one is paid for; the player should hold on to them. Big victories are rare, and they cost something terrible. Skirmish stays balanced; the campaign's power gap comes from context: the Regency holds orbit (strikes, glassing beams mid-mission), campaign-only Regency assets, ARC tech that isn't invented yet, reinforcements that stop coming.

## Structure

- **One campaign, mostly ARC,** with short **interludes** from other sides (Halo 2's Arbiter): rebels in Era II, the Regency in Eras III–IV.
- **Faction swap (unlockable, non-canon):** replay any finished mission, with the same objectives and enemy, using any faction's army.
- **Build rule:** mission scripts refer to **roles and objectives** ("the player's commander", "6 medium ground units", "hold zone"), never unit ids, so any faction can play them.
- **Before printing** (Era II up to the first fabrication prototypes): the player still has a Commander, but it has no fabrication gun. Nothing is built on the map. Reinforcements arrive by transport: they shift-warp in and land, or fly in from off the map, and transports leave by taking off. The Commander's printing arrives with the prototypes, as a jump the player feels.
- **Future mechanic:** cities with populations that can be nuked or glassed; a war-map screen where worlds go dark.

## Storytelling rules

1. Show, never tell. No character remarks on a clue.
2. Plant clue A and clue B eras apart, through different channels (lore text, sound, silhouette, gameplay, terrain).
3. Every clue has an ordinary explanation where it first appears.
4. In-world writers guess, and some are wrong.
5. Reveals confirm what attentive players already suspected.

**Example pairs:**

| Clue A (early) | Clue B (later) |
|---|---|
| The Commander's build sound | The Engine's voice, deeper and slower |
| A tech-division compound on an Era II map that nobody fights over *(proposed)* | Its crest on the first Commander's hull plate |
| "Earth wheat took the first season" | Hale's crops take a decade |
| ARC T3 factory gantry silhouette | Engine node silhouette |
| The Regency glasses one colony, skip a bigger one | Only the first had a fabrication yard |
| Rebel weapon glow color | The Seals' color |
| A straight ridge under an Asterian forest | The straight Meridian seam |

Every plant and its payoff should be tracked, along with the file or asset it lives in, so reworks don't break a thread.
