# Meridian Conflict: Lore One-Pager

This is the **truth layer**. None of it is ever stated on screen. Players see only the surface: clues they piece together themselves. Names marked *(placeholder)* are still open.

## The premise

Humanity was barely spacefaring when it found a relic in outer-moon ice. That's **Contact**, year **0 AC** (After Contact). The relic, **the Seed**, gave humanity faster-than-light travel and a star map. The map led to **Asteria**, a perfect world. Earth and Asteria unified as **the Asterian Reach**, with **Asterian Reach Command (ARC)** as its military. The map also marked **Meridia**, a frontier ore world. Only the Reach's top tech divisions know it holds ruins.

**The secret:** the **Precursors** *(placeholder)* built worlds. Asteria is one of their gardens. Meridia is their **Foundry**: a world-sized machine with a planet's crust grown over it. **The Meridian** is the seam, pole to pole, where its shell breaks the surface. The **Replication Engine** is the Foundry's working machinery. It is still carrying out its last orders, and nobody is left to stop it. Human fabrication (the Commander, factories, reclaim) is copied from it.

## Factions (3 at launch)

| Faction | Wants | Look and feel |
|---|---|---|
| **ARC** | To hold the Reach, crush the rebels and weaponize the ruins | Light and dark metal plating, blue weapons, disciplined; the superpower that falls |
| **The rebels** *(placeholder name)*: fringe colonies, militias, terror cells | Independence. They struck the ruins first and use them without understanding them | Rust, hazard yellow, salvage-heavy, cheap and scrappy |
| **The Naga** *(soldiers' name; their own name is open)* | Survival. Their ancestors outlived the Engine by glassing their own worlds; to them, fabrication is plague | Living, grown technology, black and red. Afraid, not fanatical; they have doubters and politics |
| **DLC: the Guardians** *(Solace, placeholder)* | To keep the Foundry sealed | Golden, crystal, very few, fading; strong individually, not gods. Built the **Seals**, crystal spires in Meridia's orbit |

**Why the Naga glass worlds:** it's quarantine. They target fabrication, not people. The tech that keeps humanity alive is the reason it's being burned.

## Worlds

**Earth** (old, political) · **Asteria** (garden capital, the last stand) · **Hale** (ice mining; Serac maps) · **an ocean colony** (naval; Twin Shoals; the first world glassed) · **a desert moon** (the rebels' heartland) · **Meridia** (the Foundry; Meridian Basin, The Crucible).

## The campaign timeline

Two short eras of peace, then a long war. Roughly a quarter of the missions come before the Arrival, and the rest are the war.

**Before the war**

| Era | Years (AC) | What the player lives through |
|---|---|---|
| I. The Reach | 0–140 | Montage-paced, with a few missions only: Contact, FTL, the land rush on Asteria between Earth's blocs, unification, ARC founded, colonies spread, fabrication appears. ARC is the superpower. |
| II. The Troubles | 140–175 | Meridia settled (~150); the rebel insurgency; Twin Shoals, the Blackridge Line. Rebels field strange weapons. It ends with digging on Meridia waking something that signals. |

**The war**

| Era | Years (AC) | What the player lives through | The small win |
|---|---|---|---|
| III. The Arrival | 175–177 | The Naga arrive. The first battle is a massacre; ARC shells bounce. Meridia evacuated under fire. The ocean colony is glassed within weeks. | The first Naga unit killed, at a cost that makes clear how bad the odds are. |
| IV. The Retreat | 177–182 | World after world, delaying actions and evacuations. The navy is bled white. ARC and the rebels form an uneasy truce, city by city. | A city gets out whole. A glassing run is diverted. The rebels show up when no one asked them to. |
| V. The Siege of Hale | 182–185 | An ice world that refuses to fall. Years of holding out in the mountains and under the glaciers. Morale lives on Hale. | A Naga capital ship brought down. Hale holding at all. A Naga doubter makes contact. |
| VI. The Meridian | 185–188 | A desperate raid back to Meridia, down into the seam, to steal what the Foundry knows. The Seals; why the Naga burn. It works, and it proves the Naga right. Hale falls. | The first real counter-punch: Foundry-derived weapons crack Naga shields. |
| VII. The Fall of Asteria | 188–190 | The fortress world. Cities, populations, nukes on overrun districts. The war-map goes dark around it. | Every evacuation ship that clears orbit. |
| VIII. Embers | 190+ | The Engine wakes. The final reveal (Asteria was built). What's left of every side decides what to do with the Foundry. | Open: the ending. |

**Tone:** Halo's Fall of Reach. ARC is the superpower in Eras I–II, so the player feels strong. Then the Naga hit far harder, fast. From the Arrival on, the war is desperate: objectives are hold, evacuate, delay, lives saved. Hope comes from **small wins**: one ship, one city, one more month. Each one is earned and each one is paid for; the player should hold on to them. Big victories are rare, and they cost something terrible. Skirmish stays balanced; the campaign's power gap comes from context: Naga hold orbit (strikes, glassing beams mid-mission), campaign-only Naga assets, locked ARC tech, reinforcements that stop coming.

## Structure

- **One campaign, mostly ARC,** with short **interludes** from other sides (Halo 2's Arbiter): rebels in Era II, Naga in Eras III–IV.
- **Faction swap (unlockable, non-canon):** replay any finished mission, with the same objectives and enemy, using any faction's army.
- **Build rule:** mission scripts refer to **roles and objectives** ("the player's commander", "6 medium ground units", "hold zone"), never unit ids, so any faction can play them.
- **Future mechanic:** cities with populations that can be nuked or glassed; a war-map screen where worlds go dark.

## Storytelling rules

1. Show, never tell. No character remarks on a clue.
2. Plant clue A and clue B eras apart, through different channels (lore text, sound, silhouette, gameplay, terrain).
3. Every clue has an ordinary explanation where it first appears.
4. In-world writers guess, and some are wrong.
5. Reveals confirm what attentive players already suspected.

**Example pairs:**

| Clue A (early) | Clue B (later) |
|---|---|
| The Commander's build sound | The Engine's voice, deeper and slower |
| Corvus Foundries founded 1 AC | Contact dated 0 AC |
| The Seed as a museum piece | Its geometry cut into the Meridian walls |
| "Earth wheat took the first season" | Hale's crops take a decade |
| ARC T3 factory gantry silhouette | Engine node silhouette |
| The Naga never reclaim; they burn wrecks | Reclaim is what the Engine does |
| The Naga glass one colony, skip a bigger one | Only the first had a fabrication yard |
| Rebel weapon glow color | The Seals' color |
| A straight ridge under an Asterian forest | The straight Meridian seam |

Every plant and its payoff should be tracked, along with the file or asset it lives in, so reworks don't break a thread.
